# Chapter 7: Chamber Wall Dynamics, Seasoning & Memory Effects

## 7.1 Wall Recombination Coefficients
Vacuum chamber walls act as vast sinks or sources for reactive radicals:
- The probability that a radical recombines upon wall impact is the **recombination coefficient** ($\gamma_{\text{wall}}$).
- For atomic fluorine on bare aluminum, $\gamma_{\text{wall}} \approx 10^{-3}$; on fluorocarbon-coated walls, $\gamma_{\text{wall}} \approx 10^{-4}$.
- A $10\times$ decrease in wall recombination doubles the radical concentration in the bulk plasma, altering etch rate and profile taper.

## 7.2 The Chamber Seasoning Cycle
When a chamber is cleaned, bare ceramic walls absorb radicals aggressively. Tool recipes run **dummy seasoning wafers** (5 to 15 wafers) to coat the walls with a stable polymer before processing production lots.
- **Yttria ($\text{Y}_2\text{O}_3$) Coatings:** Modern etch chambers use plasma-sprayed yttria or yttrium oxyfluoride ($\text{YOF}$) liners, which resist halogen radical erosion $10\times$ longer than anodized aluminum and eliminate heavy metal flake defectivity.
