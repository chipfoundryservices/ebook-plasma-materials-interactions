# Chapter 4: Fluorocarbon Passivation & The Etch-to-Deposition Balance

## 4.1 The Fluorocarbon Surface Layer
In dielectric etch reactors using gases like $\text{C}_4\text{F}_8$, $\text{C}_4\text{F}_6$, and $\text{CF}_4$, a steady-state fluorocarbon polymer film ($\text{CF}_x$) deposits continuously on all surfaces.
- **Film Thickness Equilibrium:** Governed by the competition between radical deposition flux ($J_{\text{poly}} \propto [\text{CF}_2]$) and ion sputtering/reaction consumption ($J_{\text{etch}} \propto J_{\text{ion}} \sqrt{E_{\text{ion}}}$).

## 4.2 Selective Oxide vs. Silicon Etching
The fluorocarbon layer provides unprecedented selectivity ($>50:1$) when etching Silicon Dioxide ($\text{SiO}_2$) stopping on Silicon ($\text{Si}$):
1. **On $\text{SiO}_2$:** Oxygen atoms liberated from the substrate lattice react with the fluorocarbon polymer to form volatile byproducts ($\text{CO}$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{COF}_2$). This thins the polymer layer ($<1\text{nm}$), allowing ions to penetrate and etch $\text{SiO}_2$ continuously.
2. **On $\text{Si}$:** The silicon substrate contains no oxygen to consume carbon. The fluorocarbon layer thickens ($>2\text{–}4\text{nm}$), blocking low-energy ions and completely stopping etch progress.
