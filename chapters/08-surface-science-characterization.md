# Chapter 8: Surface Science Characterization: In-Situ XPS & SIMS

## 8.1 In-Situ X-Ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
To understand surface modification without exposure to atmospheric oxygen and water, high-end research reactors connect plasma chambers to ultra-high vacuum ($<10^{-9}\text{ Torr}$) XPS analysis chambers via magnetic transfer arms.
- **Core-Level Shifts:** Chemical shifts in the $\text{C } 1s$ spectrum resolve $\text{C-C}$, $\text{C-CF}_x$, $\text{CF}$, $\text{CF}_2$, and $\text{CF}_3$ bonding fractions in real time.
- **Angle-Resolved XPS (ARXPS):** By varying photoelectron takeoff angle from $15^\\circ$ to $90^\\circ$, engineers construct non-destructive depth profiles of the modified top $1\text{–}6\text{ nm}$ layer.

## 8.2 Time-of-Flight SIMS (ToF-SIMS)
ToF-SIMS bombards the surface with pulsed bismuth ($\text{Bi}_3^+$) cluster ions, detecting trace atomic and molecular fragments with sub-parts-per-billion sensitivity:
- Traces halogen penetration depth into nanosheet sidewalls.
- Detects ultra-trace metal contamination (Fe, Cr, Ni, Cu) originating from chamber wall sputtering.
