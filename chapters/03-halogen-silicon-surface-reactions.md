# Chapter 3: Halogen-Silicon Surface Reactions: F, Cl & Br Dynamics

## 3.1 Fluorine Chemisorption ($\text{F}$ / $\text{CF}_4$ / $\text{SF}_6$)
Fluorine atoms react spontaneously with silicon without requiring ion bombardment:
- A fluorosilyl reaction layer ($\text{SiF}_x$, $x=1,2,3,4$) spontaneously forms up to $1.5\text{–}2.5\text{ nm}$ deep.
- Once $\text{SiF}_3$ coverage is saturated, room-temperature thermal reaction forms volatile **Silicon Tetrafluoride** ($\text{SiF}_4$), which desorbs spontaneously.
- **Consequence:** Pure fluorine plasmas etch silicon isotropically, making them ideal for sacrificial layer removal but unsuitable for anisotropic vertical gate etching.

## 3.2 Chlorine Chemisorption ($\text{Cl}_2$)
Chlorine atoms chemisorb onto silicon, forming a stable monolayer of $\text{SiCl}$ and $\text{SiCl}_2$:
- Unlike fluorine, chlorine **does not react spontaneously** with silicon at room temperature ($T < 150^\\circ\\text{C}$).
- Etching **requires ion bombardment** to drive desorption of volatile Silicon Tetrachloride ($\text{SiCl}_4$) and Silicon Dichloride ($\text{SiCl}_2$).
- Unbombarded sidewalls remain pristine, providing clean anisotropic vertical profiles.

## 3.3 Bromine Chemisorption ($\text{HBr}$)
Bromine atoms are larger ($r_{\text{Br}} = 1.14\text{ Å}$ vs. $r_{\text{F}} = 0.71\text{ Å}$) and less electronegative:
- Zero spontaneous chemical etch rate.
- Forms non-volatile silicon oxy-bromide ($\text{SiO}_x\text{Br}_y$) passivation on sidewalls when trace oxygen is present.
- Industry standard for polysilicon and silicon nanosheet gate etching with $>100:1$ selectivity to ultra-thin gate oxides.
